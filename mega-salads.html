Composition

What each layer does

Usual base

Fresh vegetables
One romaine heart · half a bell pepper · half a cucumber · some kale.
Legumes
Half a can of mixed six beans · about half a cup [120 mL] of rinsed lupini.
Protein
One can of water-packed tuna.
Extras
Pickled beets · marinated olives.
Often added
About half a cup [120 mL] of goat/cow-milk feta.

Variations:

  • tomato, a full can of beans, red cabbage, spinach, two or three boiled eggs, or seasoned grilled chicken.
  • These are options across the journal, not ingredients assumed in every photo.
Romaine, kale & spinachVolume + leaf micronutrients

Water and fiber build the bowl’s volume. Leafy greens contribute folate, vitamin K and carotenoids; kale and spinach vary the micronutrient matrix.

Six-bean blendCarbohydrate + plant protein

Mixed legumes supply starch for energy, plant protein and fiber. Half versus a full can changes the meal substantially; use drained weight and the package label.

Lupini beansProtein-rich legume layer

Lupini contributes a different protein-to-carbohydrate balance from the mixed-bean base. Use commercially prepared edible lupini, rinsed and drained, for the recorded half-cup [120 mL] addition.

Water-packed tunaLean muscle-repair protein

Drained tuna supplies essential amino acids for muscle-protein synthesis. Species and can size change the matrix; the calculator uses a generic drained-water-packed reference.

Grilled chickenAlternative protein anchor

Cooked breast supplies essential amino acids with relatively little fat before oil or marinade. Swap it for tuna in the calculator, or log the extra portion separately when both are used.

Boiled eggsProtein + yolk nutrients

Two or three eggs add protein, fat and choline. They are an addition to the base, not nutritionally equivalent to the same weight of tuna or chicken.

Goat/cow-milk fetaDairy protein + calcium

Feta adds dairy protein, calcium, fat and salt. The milk blend and brine vary by product; the calculator’s 75 g half-cup [120 mL] assumption is adjustable.

Bell pepper, cucumber & tomatoCrunch + vitamin C

Pepper contributes vitamin C, which improves absorption of plant-derived nonheme iron. Cucumber contributes water and crunch; tomato adds another vegetable and carotenoid layer.

Pickled beets & red cabbageColor + different plant compounds

Beets contribute betalain pigments; red cabbage contributes anthocyanins. Pickled beets are not a standardized sports-nitrate product, and sweetened brine changes carbohydrate.

Olives & avocado dressingFlavor + energy-dense fats

These supply fat and help make the meal satisfying. Dietary fat supports absorption of fat-soluble nutrients; oil contributes much more energy per spoon than leafy vegetables.

Useful ingredient interactions

Macro synergy — protein + legumes: tuna, chicken, eggs or dairy provide essential amino acids; beans add carbohydrate fuel, fiber and additional protein. Recovery depends on the day’s total intake and training load.

Pepper + plant iron: vitamin C helps absorption of nonheme iron from plant foods. This is a food pairing, not a treatment for iron deficiency.

Vegetables + fat: avocado, oil, olives and feta provide fat alongside fat-soluble nutrients. More oil increases energy; it is not required to keep adding oil for the pairing to work.

Rinsing + brine: draining and rinsing beans, lupini and brined ingredients can reduce some surface salt; the remaining sodium depends on the product. Feta, olives and pickles still contribute to the meal’s salt.

A solid recovery meal

After the session

  • Use as a solid-food recovery meal: a protein anchor for muscle repair, legumes for carbohydrate fuel, and vegetables for meal volume and micronutrient variety.
  • It sits alongside the recorded post-training liquid sequence, not as an extra mandatory vector.
Review the liquid sequence →

Strength & recovery

  • Tuna, chicken and eggs each supply complete protein; they can be alternatives or additions.
  • Change the portion to fit the day’s protein intake.
  • Extra beans increase carbohydrate and fiber; a higher-demand training day may need additional carbohydrate beyond this bowl.

Energy & body composition

  • The greens provide volume while oil, avocado, feta and eggs determine much of the energy density.
  • Keep the same recipe framework and adjust portions to the day’s target.
  • A salad with the full dressing is a substantial meal.

Rest-day continuity

  • The same food base can remain on a rest day; align legumes, protein and dressing with appetite, the daily plan and digestive comfort.
  • The photos document meals, not a proven change in body composition.
