Post-Workout Recovery Mega Salads

Composition

What each layer does

Usual base: one romaine heart, half a can of mixed six beans, half a bell pepper, half a cucumber, some kale, pickled beets, marinated olives, one can of water-packed tuna, about half a cup of rinsed lupini, and often about half a cup of goat/cow-milk feta.

Variations: tomato, a full can of beans, red cabbage, spinach, two or three boiled eggs, or seasoned grilled chicken. These are options across the journal, not ingredients assumed in every photo.

Romaine, kale & spinachVolume + leaf micronutrients

Water and fiber build the bowl’s volume. Leafy greens contribute folate, vitamin K and carotenoids; kale and spinach vary the micronutrient matrix.

Six-bean blendCarbohydrate + plant protein

Mixed legumes supply starch for energy, plant protein and fiber. Half versus a full can changes the meal substantially; use drained weight and the package label.

Lupini beansProtein-rich legume layer

Lupini contributes a different protein-to-carbohydrate balance from the mixed-bean base. Use commercially prepared edible lupini, rinsed and drained, for the recorded half-cup addition.

Water-packed tunaLean muscle-repair protein

Drained tuna supplies essential amino acids for muscle-protein synthesis. Species and can size change the matrix; the calculator uses a generic drained-water-packed reference.

Grilled chickenAlternative protein anchor

Cooked breast supplies essential amino acids with relatively little fat before oil or marinade. Swap it for tuna in the calculator, or log the extra portion separately when both are used.

Boiled eggsProtein + yolk nutrients

Two or three eggs add protein, fat and choline. They are an addition to the base, not nutritionally equivalent to the same weight of tuna or chicken.

Goat/cow-milk fetaDairy protein + calcium

Feta adds dairy protein, calcium, fat and salt. The milk blend and brine vary by product; the calculator’s 75 g half-cup assumption is adjustable.

Bell pepper, cucumber & tomatoCrunch + vitamin C

Pepper contributes vitamin C, which improves absorption of plant-derived nonheme iron. Cucumber contributes water and crunch; tomato adds another vegetable and carotenoid layer.

Pickled beets & red cabbageColor + different plant compounds

Beets contribute betalain pigments; red cabbage contributes anthocyanins. Pickled beets are not a standardized sports-nitrate supplement, and sweetened brine changes carbohydrate.

Olives & avocado dressingFlavor + energy-dense fats

These supply fat and help make the meal satisfying. Dietary fat supports absorption of fat-soluble nutrients; oil contributes much more energy per spoon than leafy vegetables.

Useful ingredient interactions

Macro synergy — protein + legumes: tuna, chicken, eggs or dairy provide essential amino acids; beans add carbohydrate fuel, fiber and additional protein. Recovery depends on the day’s total intake and training load.

Pepper + plant iron: vitamin C helps absorption of nonheme iron from plant foods. This is a food pairing, not a treatment for iron deficiency.

Vegetables + fat: avocado, oil, olives and feta provide fat alongside fat-soluble nutrients. More oil increases energy; it is not required to keep adding oil for the pairing to work.

Rinsing + brine: draining and rinsing beans, lupini and brined ingredients can reduce some surface salt; the remaining sodium depends on the product. Feta, olives and pickles still contribute to the meal’s salt.

Nutrition model

Portions change the purpose

Editable whole-bowl estimates, not photo-measured nutrition. Starting portions are assumptions: half a can of beans = 150 g drained; half a cup feta = 75 g; half a cup lupini = 80 g; tuna = 120 g drained. Replace them with your actual weights.

Edit the base ingredients & optional vegetables

Two dressings · your choice

Choose by appetite, texture and flavor, independently of the salad ingredients. “Heavy” and “Light” describe creamy versus fluid texture; the amounts used determine calories.

Heavy · Creamy Avocado

Your confirmed serving: the entire batch per salad, when used. One avocado is modeled as 150 g edible flesh; adjust for its size. Oil starts at 2½ tablespoons within your corrected 2–3 tablespoon range. About half a cup of white vinegar completes the whole-bowl dressing; its acidity balances the thick avocado paste. The calculator includes a generic 120 g vinegar estimate.

Light · Virgin Olive Oil & Balsamic Glaze

Virgin olive oil, balsamic glaze and white wine vinegar make the fluid vinaigrette option. The oil provides fat and the glaze adds sweetness; vinegar adds acidity. Your usual amount is four to five tablespoons each of virgin olive oil and balsamic glaze: about 60–75 mL each using a 15 mL tablespoon. This fits the small coffee-cup estimate, rather than a 240 mL measuring cup. The calculator starts at the midpoint, 4½ tablespoons (67.5 mL) each; adjust to what you actually used. White wine vinegar stays at about 120 mL.

Oil is modeled at 0.91 g per mL. Glaze energy is estimated from its label carbohydrate; other label nutrients may alter the result. Enter zero for an ingredient omitted. Unmeasured oil or glaze leaves the dressed total incomplete.

Your mega bowl often leaves liquid at the bottom. Prepared dressing is not necessarily all eaten. Reduce this percentage only as a rough estimate; oil and vinegar may separate, so leftover liquid volume does not establish leftover calories. Avocado dressing is thicker and uses only 2–3 tablespoons oil.

How the estimate is calculated

Each ingredient’s entered grams are multiplied by rounded representative per-100 g food values, then summed. An egg is modeled as 50 g edible weight; one tablespoon oil as 14 g. Half a cup vinegar is modeled as 120 g. Macronutrient values include total carbohydrate and fiber separately; fiber is not an additional carbohydrate amount. Mixed beans use a generic cooked-bean approximation, not your brand’s six-bean label. Feta, lupini brine, sweetened beets, marinades and tuna species can differ.

Use package labels and weighed portions for tracking. Sodium and exact micronutrient totals are not calculated without product information. Photo cards show the separately submitted archive estimates, labeled with their unresolved dressing status; they are not measurements derived from the photos.

A solid recovery meal

After the session

Use as a solid-food recovery meal: a protein anchor for muscle repair, legumes for carbohydrate fuel, and vegetables for meal volume and micronutrient variety. It sits alongside the recorded post-training liquid sequence, not as an extra mandatory vector.

Review the liquid sequence →

Strength & recovery

Tuna, chicken and eggs each supply complete protein; they can be alternatives or additions. Change the portion to fit the day’s protein intake. Extra beans increase carbohydrate and fiber; a higher-demand training day may need additional carbohydrate beyond this bowl.

Energy & body composition

The greens provide volume while oil, avocado, feta and eggs determine much of the energy density. Keep the same recipe framework and adjust portions to the day’s target. A salad with the full dressing is a substantial meal.

Rest-day continuity

The same food base can remain on a rest day; align legumes, protein and dressing with appetite, the daily plan and digestive comfort. The photos document meals, not a proven change in body composition.
