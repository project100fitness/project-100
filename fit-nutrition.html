The meal foundation

Mega salads, smoothies and the other meals bring together nutrient-rich ingredients: vegetables, fruit and beans for fiber and micronutrients; eggs, fish, poultry and dairy-based protein for essential amino acids; and avocado and olive oil for mostly unsaturated fats. Variety across the day broadens the nutrient mix. Protein anchors the meals, while carbohydrate foods and fats contribute energy for the training routine.

Dressing & portion details

Heavy · Creamy avocado

1 whole avocado + 2–3 tbsp avocado oil + white vinegar to taste.

Modeled with 130–200 g edible avocado. White vinegar to taste is excluded from this approximate range.

Light · Olive oil and balsamic glaze

4–5 tbsp virgin olive oil + 4–5 tbsp balsamic glaze + approximately 120 mL white wine vinegar.

Glaze uses a provisional 7–12 g carbohydrate per tablespoon; its label is needed to refine the estimate. “Light” describes texture, not fewer calories.

Prepared versus eaten: totals include the whole prepared dressing batch. Liquid left at the bottom means actual intake is lower by an unknown amount; oil separation prevents a reliable calorie deduction from liquid volume alone.

All photos use a 15-inch mixing bowl. Ingredient weights are estimated because fill depth and composition vary.

Portion rules: 4 eggs in an egg-led bowl without legumes · 2 eggs with mixed beans and lupini · 1 whole can of beans with tuna when eggs and lupini are absent.

Estimated ranges · full bowl + prepared dressing · open a card for ingredient assumptions. Unidentified protein toppings are excluded rather than assigned a guessed species.

Nutrition references and remaining label checks

Representative food coefficients are rounded generic values; assumed portions are listed in each card. Salad totals are estimates, not values measured from the photo or personalized targets. Egg-led recipes use four eggs (about 200 g edible); mixed-bean and lupini recipes use two eggs (about 100 g). Tuna-and-bean versions without eggs or lupini use one whole can of beans. Drained can weight is provisionally 240–360 g until the label is supplied.

Sodium depends on feta, olives, pickled beets, canned foods and seasonings. Labels are required for numerical sodium, sugar, saturated fat and micronutrient totals.

Ingredients · What They Contribute

Explore the foods behind the salads, smoothies, wraps and protein treats. Each ingredient has a main category plus its overlapping nutritional roles.

🧬 Protein MatrixEssential amino acids & complementary protein10

Eggs, fish, poultry, meat and protein powders contribute essential amino acids. Collagen has a different profile; portion and the day’s total intake matter more than stacking every source.

Water-packed tunaLean muscle-repair protein

Complete protein · B12

Drained tuna supplies essential amino acids for muscle-protein synthesis. Species and drained can weight change the nutrient totals; the salad cards use representative values and estimated portions.

Grilled chickenAlternative protein anchor

Complete protein · B vitamins

Cooked breast supplies essential amino acids with relatively little fat before oil or marinade. It can replace tuna or accompany other protein foods; include every portion actually used in the bowl.

Boiled eggsProtein + yolk nutrients

Complete protein · choline · fat

Four eggs are used in egg-led bowls without legumes; two are used with mixed beans and lupini. Eggs add protein, fat and choline. They are an addition to the base, not nutritionally equivalent to the same weight of tuna or chicken.

Protein powders · Hexapro & ISO GOLDConcentrated protein

Essential amino acids · convenient protein

The reels name Hexapro in the berries smoothie and fudge, and ISO GOLD in the chocolate-blueberry blend. These supply essential amino acids in a convenient form. Scoop weights and protein content vary by product; a blend does not establish a guaranteed eight-hour effect.

Egg-white powderProtein without the yolk

Complete protein · little fat

The chocolate-blueberry recipe includes egg-white powder. It adds complete protein with little fat. Its powder weight is distinct from the liquid egg whites used in the omelette; use a product intended for the chosen preparation.

Marine & bovine collagenAdditional peptide protein

Peptide protein · different amino-acid profile

The fudge caption names marine collagen; the chocolate-blueberry caption names bovine collagen. Collagen adds protein but has a different amino-acid profile from whey, eggs or milk and should not replace those as the main complete-protein anchor.

Whole eggs & liquid egg whitesComplete protein with adjustable fat

Complete protein · choline

Both savory recipes use eggs; the omelette also includes liquid whites. Whole eggs contribute choline and yolk fat, while whites increase protein with less fat. These meal recipes have their own egg amounts, separate from the salad rules.

Turkey bacon & crispy pork bellySavory protein and fat

Protein · fat · sodium

Turkey bacon appears in the wraps and pork belly in the omelette. Fat and sodium vary with the cut and preparation. Draining rendered fat changes the dish, but does not make pork belly equivalent to lean chicken breast.

Beef steaksProtein, iron and zinc

Complete protein · iron · zinc

The air-fryer reel lists Montreal-seasoned steaks. Beef supplies essential amino acids, heme iron, zinc and B12. Cut, cooked weight and seasoning determine energy, fat and sodium; the reel’s protein ranges are not measurements for every plate.

Chicken breasts, legs, wings & cutletsDifferent poultry options

Complete protein · variable fat

The reel lists tandoori/lemon-pepper breasts, whole legs, wings, tenders and cutlets. Poultry contributes protein and B vitamins, but skin, breading and cut change the fat and carbohydrate content. Air frying is a preparation method, not proof that every option is low fat.

🥑 Fats & EnergyUnsaturated fats, texture & energy density3

Avocado, olive oil, chia and peanut ingredients contribute fats in different proportions. Oils are concentrated energy sources; chia and peanut foods also bring fiber or protein.

Olives, avocado & dressing oilsFlavor + energy-dense fats

Mostly unsaturated fat · energy

Olives, avocado, avocado oil and extra-virgin olive oil supply fat and help make the meal satisfying. The Heavy dressing uses avocado and avocado oil; the Light dressing uses olive oil with balsamic glaze. Dietary fat supports absorption of fat-soluble nutrients; oil contributes much more energy per spoon than leafy vegetables.

Chia seeds · soakedFiber, gel texture and plant fats

Fiber · ALA fat · gel texture

The chocolate-blueberry blend includes soaked chia. Chia supplies fiber and the plant omega-3 ALA; hydration produces the thick gel texture. Half a cup of soaked gel does not mean half a cup of dry seeds, so dry seed weight is needed for nutrition totals.

PB&Me powdered peanut butterPeanut flavor and plant protein

Plant protein · peanut flavor

PB&Me appears in the berries smoothie and chocolate fudge. Removing much of the peanut oil creates a powder with a different fat-to-protein balance from regular peanut butter. Chocolate varieties can have different sugar and calorie values.

⚡ Carbohydrates & FuelFruit, tortillas & mixed meal energy3

Fruit, legumes, tortillas and meal coatings contribute carbohydrate. Sources differ in sugars, starch and fiber, so complex carbohydrate is not a label for every item in this group.

Berries & wild blueberriesFruit, fiber and plant pigments

Carbohydrate · fiber · plant pigments

The berries smoothie, chocolate-blueberry blend and fudge topping use berries. They add carbohydrate, fiber and anthocyanin pigments. Whole blended berries retain their fiber; blending is different from straining juice.

Protein & high-fiber tortillasPortable meal base

Carbohydrate · fiber · some protein

The wraps reel names POM Protein tortillas; the omelette names Bon Matin Keto tortillas. They hold the filling and contribute carbohydrate, fiber and some protein. Their added fiber and portion size determine the totals; the word keto alone does not describe the whole meal.

Ribs, fish & spring rollsMixed meal options

Protein · carbohydrate · variable fat

The air-fryer menu also includes ribs and crispy fish with spring rolls. Ribs contribute protein and fat; fish contributes protein, with omega-3 content depending on species. Rolls and coatings add carbohydrate and oil. These items have different roles and should be accounted for individually.

🌾 Fiber & DigestionLegumes, cereal & meal structure4

Beans, lupini, chia, berries and bran add different fiber types. Their food structure and fluid content affect texture and digestive comfort; toppings and thickeners have separate roles.

Six-bean blendCarbohydrate + plant protein

Plant protein · carbohydrate · fiber

Mixed legumes supply starch for energy, plant protein and fiber. Half versus a full can changes the meal substantially; use drained weight and the package label.

Lupini beansProtein-rich legume layer

Plant protein · fiber

Lupini contributes a different protein-to-carbohydrate balance from the mixed-bean base. Use commercially prepared edible lupini, rinsed and drained, for the recorded half-cup addition.

All-Bran BudsFiber-rich topping and crunch

Fiber · carbohydrate · crunch

The fudge reel lists All-Bran Buds with frozen berries. The cereal adds fiber and texture, plus carbohydrate. Count the amount added to the batch and how it is divided between jars; it is not interchangeable with plain oats.

Xanthan gum & Himalayan saltTexture and seasoning

Thickening · sodium seasoning

The fudge recipe uses a pinch of each; the chocolate-blueberry blend also lists salt. Xanthan thickens the mixture, while salt adds sodium and taste. Pink color does not turn salt into a balanced potassium/magnesium electrolyte source.

🥛 Dairy & CalciumMilk protein, calcium & alternative bases4

Feta, cheddar and protein milk provide dairy protein and calcium. Almond beverage and tofu spread are included for comparison: their protein and calcium depend on the actual product.

Goat/cow-milk fetaDairy protein + calcium

Dairy protein · calcium · fat · sodium

Feta adds dairy protein, calcium, fat and salt. The milk blend and brine vary by product; a half-cup volume can vary in weight depending on crumbling and packing.

Unsweetened almond milkBlending liquid

Fluid · fortified nutrients where present

The chocolate-blueberry reel records 3 cups. It adds fluid and texture but is generally a modest protein source. Calcium and vitamin D depend on fortification; shake the carton and check its label rather than counting it as equivalent to dairy protein milk.

Ultra-filtered protein milkDairy protein, fluid and calcium

Complete protein · calcium · fluid

The fudge recipe names Neilson protein milk. It contributes whey/casein protein and calcium, alongside milk fat and remaining carbohydrate. Use the actual milk label; almond beverage and ultra-filtered dairy milk are different nutritional bases.

Old cheddar & tofu spreadFilling flavor and texture

Protein · calcium · creamy texture

The wrap caption names old cheddar and Fontaine Santé tofu spread. Cheddar contributes protein, calcium, fat and sodium; the spread adds creamy texture and its own soy-based nutrient mix. Use each product’s serving label to account for the meal.

🥬 Plant Micronutrients & FlavorVegetables, cacao & food pairings6

Leafy greens, colorful vegetables, berries and cacao broaden the micronutrient and plant-compound mix. Seasonings add flavor; pickled and salted ingredients also affect sodium.

Romaine, kale & spinachVolume + leaf micronutrients

Fiber · folate · vitamin K

Water and fiber build the bowl’s volume. Leafy greens contribute folate, vitamin K and carotenoids; kale and spinach vary the micronutrient matrix.

Bell pepper, cucumber & tomatoCrunch + vitamin C

Vitamin C · water · plant compounds

Pepper contributes vitamin C, which improves absorption of plant-derived nonheme iron. Cucumber contributes water and crunch; tomato adds another vegetable and carotenoid layer.

Pickled beets & red cabbageColor + different plant compounds

Fiber · plant pigments

Beets contribute betalain pigments; red cabbage contributes anthocyanins. Pickled beets are not a standardized sports-nitrate supplement, and sweetened brine changes carbohydrate.

Raw cacaoChocolate flavor and food minerals

Fiber · magnesium · flavor

Cacao appears in the chocolate-blueberry blend. It adds cocoa flavor, fiber and minerals such as magnesium. Flavanol content varies with processing; this recipe does not establish a measured nitric-oxide or recovery effect.

Romaine, pickled turnips & onionVegetable texture and flavor

Vegetable variety · sodium in brine

The wraps use romaine and Cedar pickled turnips; the omelette includes yellow onion. These add vegetable variety. Pickling does not guarantee live probiotics, and brine adds sodium; cooking oil is counted separately from the onion.

Sriracha & savory drizzlesSeasoning

Flavor · label-dependent sodium

The wrap reel names Huy Fong Sriracha; the omelette lists a zero-sugar savory drizzle. These add flavor, with sodium and other ingredients determined by the label. Zero sugar does not establish zero calories or a metabolic benefit.

Useful ingredient interactions

Macro synergy — protein + legumes: tuna, chicken, eggs or dairy provide essential amino acids; beans add carbohydrate fuel, fiber and additional protein. Recovery depends on the day’s total intake and training load.

Pepper + plant iron: vitamin C helps absorption of nonheme iron from plant foods. This is a food pairing, not a treatment for iron deficiency.

Vegetables + fat: avocado, oil, olives and feta provide fat alongside fat-soluble nutrients. More oil increases energy; it is not required to keep adding oil for the pairing to work.

Rinsing + brine: draining and rinsing beans, lupini and brined ingredients can reduce some surface salt; the remaining sodium depends on the product. Feta, olives and pickles still contribute to the meal’s salt.

Clinical notes

Mechanism
Protein, adequate energy and meal tolerance support repeatable training.
Stack interaction
Fat boluses, supplemental magnesium and vitamin C compound GI tolerance in the recorded no-gallbladder context.
Margin
Known totals are partial labels. Training supplemental Mg baseline 341 mg; options may increase it to 646 mg versus 350 mg/day supplemental UL.
Monitor
Record stool, abdominal pain and the optional items actually taken.
Stop rule
Recurrent watery stools with weight loss, oily stools or significant abdominal pain require assessment.
