The Prime Directive and the Return

PRIME DIRECTIVE

Rebuild to 100 lb skeletal muscle mass at under 15% body fat, without trading long-term health for short- term numbers. Every protocol in this book must pass three locks: it must support training, it must be tolerable day after day, and it must leave a clean monitoring trail. If a protocol fails any lock, the protocol changes — not the target.

How the target is measured (Encyclopedia Ch. 1): 100 lb is a bioimpedance-estimated skeletal muscle mass, tracked on the same device under standardised conditions — waking, post-void, pre- intake, similar hydration and meal state. It is a personal target, not a clinical threshold. Creatine, electrolytes, carbohydrate and fluid shifts can move the estimate without new muscle, so quarterly review beats weekly reaction.

Training — Analysis

Progressive overload with joint protection — the training lock.

MECHANISM

Muscle is built by progressive mechanical tension plus sufficient protein and energy, and kept by repeating that signal before the previous adaptation fades. At 46, the synthesis side still works; the recovery side is the constraint. The program therefore buys growth with volume you can recover from, not volume you can survive once.

STACK INTERACTION

With stimulant vectors: caffeine (IGNITER portion ~373 mg before tea or other sources; Earl Grey adds roughly 40–70 mg) raises output and masks fatigue — the mask is the risk. RPE must be logged honestly even on high-caffeine days.

With sleep: a short night downgrades the next session automatically. No vector compensates for that downgrade.

With joints: load progression is capped by joint feedback, not by target weights.

With hardware locks (Encyclopedia Ch. 3 & 6): supported rows, presses and machine work dominate where spine, nerve, shoulder or elbow locks require protection; free axial loading is progressed conservatively and stopped by neurological signs.

Nutrition — Analysis

Guidebook V2.0 · PDF page 6

Nutrition — Analysis

MECHANISM

Protein drives muscle protein synthesis; total energy decides whether that synthesis can run; meal timing decides tolerance. Your system holds protein constant across training and rest days and moves energy with output — the reverse (cutting protein on rest days) is the most common comeback error, because repair peaks when you are not training.

STACK INTERACTION — THE GUT IS THE BOTTLENECK

No gallbladder context: fat must be distributed across meals, not loaded. Large fat boluses plus magnesium plus vitamin C is the osmotic chain flagged in Encyclopedia Part IV — each is tolerable alone, together they produce urgent GI symptoms.

Legacy meal modules: MS-01, SB-01 and FUSION_2A are preserved legacy records in Encyclopedia Ch. 5, printed with label-check macros. They are audit history, not a verified whole-day diet prescription.

Hydration: electrolytes accompany training water, not as an afterthought; cramps are logged as data, not endured as noise.

Labs and Monitoring — Analysis

Guidebook V2.0 · PDF page 10

Labs and Monitoring — Analysis

WHY YOUR LABS NEED CONTEXT

Two published cautions govern every blood draw in Project 100 (Encyclopedia Part VI). First, biotin (425 mcg total daily) interferes with immunoassays: depending on the assay, results can read falsely high or falsely low — classically troponin can read falsely low and several hormone assays can skew. There is no universal 72-hour biotin hold: disclose it and follow assay-specific instructions. Second, creatine HCl (1.5 g daily) expands the creatinine pool, so kidney checks should include cystatin-C alongside creatinine. March laboratory results predate the May supplement stack — do not read them as a verdict on the current stack. An afternoon testosterone result is non-diagnostic; hormone interpretation needs two fasting morning draws.

Daily operator checklist

Sleep hours and awakenings recorded. Caffeine total estimated including tea.

Branch selected correctly: training grid only on training days.

R-ALA swallowed whole with water before V4 (training days).

Stool, pain, weakness and performance noted in one line each.

Optional items (Medi-C, Hey Relax) marked as taken or skipped - never assumed.

Weekly review (15 minutes)

Performance trend stable or improving? Hardware locks quiet?

One variable changed at most. Next review date set.

Red-flag checklist - for clinician discussion. Stop and seek assessment for:

Progressive hand or limb weakness, new numbness, bowel or bladder change.

Jaundice, dark urine with pale stool, fever with abdominal pain.

Recurrent watery stools with weight loss, oily stools, significant abdominal pain.

Palpitations, fainting, unusual slow pulse, muscle twitching with GI cramps (cholinergic watch).

Recurrent sweating, tremor or confusion after training (hypoglycaemia watch on R-ALA days).

Sleep disruption that does not resolve when caffeine is pulled earlier.
