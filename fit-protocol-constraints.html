3 · Hardware Locks and Clinical Constraints

Safety & Clinical

Symptoms and clinical constraints govern exercise selection before ambition does.

  • A hardware lock is a symptom or clinical constraint that changes what may be loaded: worsening hand weakness, spinal symptoms, shoulder or elbow pain, and post-cholecystectomy digestion.
  • Locks are recorded, not argued with.
  • The training programme is built around them with supported rows, presses and controlled ranges, and load is progressed only when symptoms remain stable.
  • Gallbladder removal does not mean bile production stops.
  • The liver produces bile continuously; without a gallbladder there is no storage and concentration step, so bile trickles into the intestine and large fatty meals are harder to emulsify at once.

Smaller fat portions, slower eating and meal enzymes are individual experiments. Recurrent watery stools, weight loss, significant pain, jaundice or oily stools deserve assessment.

Analysis 3A - red-flag boundaries. Progressive weakness, bowel or bladder change, saddle numbness, unexplained weight loss, jaundice, fever with abdominal pain, or chest symptoms are not training variables.

They stop the session and trigger clinical assessment. Calcium does not neutralize excess zinc, and a comforting hot drink does not establish improved fat emulsification - retired myths stay retired.
