Chapter 2 - Excavation and the return to training

Re-establish a sustainable routine after interruption before chasing peak loads.

The excavation phase finds loads, meal patterns and recovery habits that can be repeated. It cannot be measured by a claim that calories now partition perfectly into muscle. The old 4-6 week duration and 65-75 percent 1RM range were planning examples, not demonstrated personal requirements. Current readiness reflects symptoms, prior training, available time and recovery.

Reset the chronometer. A consistent sleep opportunity and morning training window make the day easier to run. Most adults should regularly obtain at least seven hours of sleep, but duration alone does not establish adequate quality. Record awakenings and daytime function. Morning caffeine may still affect sleep in a sensitive person. The IGNITER portion supplies approximately 373 mg caffeine before tea or other sources. A late rescue dose is not a remedy for insufficient sleep.

Digestive recalibration. The liver continues producing bile after cholecystectomy. Some people experience persistent diarrhea or difficulty tolerating large fatty meals, but gallbladder removal alone does not prove pancreatic enzyme insufficiency. The author reports clear digestive benefit from meal enzymes and intermittent probiotic use. Preserve that report without claiming an over-the-counter blend replaces bile or guarantees absorption. Beverage temperature follows comfort; the old mandatory hot-solvent rule is retired.

Analysis 2A - physiology of return. Early strength gains after retraining are dominated by neural recruitment and skill, not new myofibrils. Controlled repetitions, repeatable setups and manageable volume let the operator evaluate tolerance. A slower lowering phase or lighter supported exercise may help evaluation, but neither proves a nerve has healed or a spinal segment is protected. Spacing of heavy axial sessions (historically 48-72 hours) requires individual adjustment when back, elbow or shoulder symptoms are present.
