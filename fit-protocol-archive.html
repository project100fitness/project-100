Introduction - The architecture of return

PROJECT 100 records a personal return to training and a long-term ambition: 100 lb of skeletal muscle mass with body fat below 15 percent. Those numbers express the author's goal. They do not establish a

medical threshold, a guaranteed outcome or a deadline.

The confirmed recent history is heavy alcohol use from 2020 to 2025, cessation in 2025, and renewed attention to health and gym training during 2026. The current source profile reports age 46 and body weight

90.76 kg. The February publications recorded an earlier baseline of age 45, weight 197.1 lb, skeletal muscle

mass 88.2 lb and body fat 22.9 percent. These are separate dated records. This edition does not

manufacture a single exact chronology from conflicting earlier dates.

The author reports improvements in recovery, digestion and injury symptoms since rebuilding the routine.

Those experiences belong in the journey. They cannot isolate the contribution of each supplement or prove

that a large combined stack is harmless. The edition therefore gives every item a recorded dose, use status and evidence limit. Brand changes and label versions matter as much as product names.

The current gym branch uses morning workouts. Vectors V3, V1, V2, V4 and V10 describe that branch when

consumed. The rest branch uses V3, V5 and V10 when consumed, with a smaller pill routine. The evening PUMP alternative is inactive. Zinc Supreme is removed, separate copper is paused, and boron is one 3 mg

capsule. Optional half-dose Medi-C and rare Hey Relax use remain optional. This publication does not turn

them into daily requirements.

Clinical analysis - what return actually requires. Detraining after years of heavy alcohol use

combines lost contractile tissue, impaired protein synthesis, micronutrient depletion and disrupted sleep

architecture. Retraining restores responsiveness through repeatable mechanical loading, adequate energy and sleep - not through supplement volume. The 2026 ACSM position supports individualized

resistance training and regular participation; it does not validate any fixed excavation duration or load

percentage. Progress is judged against the author's own comparable measurements, function and

health.

Chapter 1 - The prime directive - what 100 means

The target is 100 lb of reported skeletal muscle mass, not lean body mass or total body weight.

Bioimpedance analysis (BIA) estimates skeletal muscle mass (SMM) from an algorithm sensitive to

intracellular and extracellular water, glycogen and electrolyte shifts. It does not directly weigh muscle tissue.

Under multi-gram osmotic supplementation, fluid compartment changes can move the estimate without any change in contractile protein. Use the same device and repeat comparable measurements - waking, post-

void, pre-intake, similar hydration and meal state - before interpreting a change as tissue growth. Track body

weight, waist, performance and symptoms alongside the estimate.

Body fat below 15 percent remains a personal aspiration, not a universal requirement for insulin sensitivity, hormone health or successful training. The historical book used competitive bodybuilding as a visual

reference. A competition division cannot establish an individual's biological ceiling. The IFBB distinguishes

Men's 212 Bodybuilding from Classic Physique; height and weight tables from Classic Physique must not be transferred to the 212 division. Retain the reference as inspiration only.

Analysis 1A - BIA physics and the 212 reference. Multifrequency BIA models total body water, then

derives fat-free mass and SMM by regression. Creatine HCl (1.5 g daily), high-dose electrolytes and carbohydrate loading all expand intracellular water and will inflate SMM readings. This is why the

chronometer standardizes measurement timing and why quarterly review beats weekly reaction. The

prime directive phrase "Train Like Two. Eat for Two" is retained as historical branding describing ambition and sustained effort. It does not prescribe twice the workload or twice the calories.

Chapter 2 - Excavation and the return to training

Re-establish a sustainable routine after interruption before chasing peak loads.

The excavation phase finds loads, meal patterns and recovery habits that can be repeated. It cannot be

measured by a claim that calories now partition perfectly into muscle. The old 4-6 week duration and 65-75

percent 1RM range were planning examples, not demonstrated personal requirements. Current readiness reflects symptoms, prior training, available time and recovery.

Reset the chronometer. A consistent sleep opportunity and morning training window make the day easier to

run. Most adults should regularly obtain at least seven hours of sleep, but duration alone does not establish

adequate quality. Record awakenings and daytime function. Morning caffeine may still affect sleep in a sensitive person. The IGNITER portion supplies approximately 373 mg caffeine before tea or other sources.

A late rescue dose is not a remedy for insufficient sleep.

Digestive recalibration. The liver continues producing bile after cholecystectomy. Some people experience persistent diarrhea or difficulty tolerating large fatty meals, but gallbladder removal alone does not prove

pancreatic enzyme insufficiency. The author reports clear digestive benefit from meal enzymes and

intermittent probiotic use. Preserve that report without claiming an over-the-counter blend replaces bile or guarantees absorption. Beverage temperature follows comfort; the old mandatory hot-solvent rule is retired.

Analysis 2A - physiology of return. Early strength gains after retraining are dominated by neural

recruitment and skill, not new myofibrils. Controlled repetitions, repeatable setups and manageable volume let the operator evaluate tolerance. A slower lowering phase or lighter supported exercise may

help evaluation, but neither proves a nerve has healed or a spinal segment is protected. Spacing of

heavy axial sessions (historically 48-72 hours) requires individual adjustment when back, elbow or shoulder symptoms are present.

Chapter 3 - Hardware locks and clinical constraints

Symptoms and clinical constraints govern exercise selection before ambition does.

A hardware lock is a symptom or clinical constraint that changes what may be loaded: worsening hand

weakness, spinal symptoms, shoulder or elbow pain, and post-cholecystectomy digestion. Locks are

recorded, not argued with. The training programme is built around them with supported rows, presses and controlled ranges, and load is progressed only when symptoms remain stable.

Gallbladder removal does not mean bile production stops. The liver produces bile continuously; without a

gallbladder there is no storage and concentration step, so bile trickles into the intestine and large fatty meals

are harder to emulsify at once. Smaller fat portions, slower eating and meal enzymes are individual experiments. Recurrent watery stools, weight loss, significant pain, jaundice or oily stools deserve

assessment.

Analysis 3A - red-flag boundaries. Progressive weakness, bowel or bladder change, saddle numbness, unexplained weight loss, jaundice, fever with abdominal pain, or chest symptoms are not

training variables. They stop the session and trigger clinical assessment. Calcium does not neutralize

excess zinc, and a comforting hot drink does not establish improved fat emulsification - retired myths stay retired.

Chapter 6 - Training mechanics and movement classes

Movement classes are defined by direction of force, joint angles and loading demands - distinct from intake vectors V1-V10.

Supported rows, presses and machine-based work dominate where hardware locks require spinal

protection. Free axial loading is progressed conservatively. Tempo, setup repeatability and pain response are recorded for every primary movement. The 2026 ACSM update supports individualized resistance

training prescription; it does not endorse any single Project 100 template for every reader.

Chapter 9 - Planning over months and years

Quarterly review beats weekly overreaction.

Quarterly body-composition review is the project cadence. The timing and scope of blood testing follow

clinical need, not the training calendar. A few comparable weigh-ins describe a week; an isolated SMM or

body-fat reading should not trigger a new diet or supplement change.

Planning horizons: weekly (performance, sleep, digestion log), quarterly (standardized BIA trend, waist,

photos under consistent light), annually (full laboratory panel timed with the clinician, medication and

supplement reconciliation). Brand changes and label versions are logged with dates, because a product

name is not a formula guarantee.

Analysis 9A. Adaptation is slow and noisy. The planning system exists to prevent the most common

error in self-directed programmes: changing three variables after one noisy measurement and learning

nothing. One change, one review window, one decision.

Chapter 10 - Connective tissue and injury considerations

Collagen, vitamin C, glycine and load progression support tolerance; no supplement proves nerve healing.

Daily collagen exposure is substantial and deliberate: 10 g marine collagen in V3, 10 g in V5 on rest days,

plus bovine collagen in legacy meal modules and glycine 3-5 g plus glutamine 5 g in V10. Vitamin C (including optional Medi-C) is the required co-factor for collagen cross-linking. The system pairs substrate

with progressive, symptom-guided loading - the stimulus that actually directs tissue adaptation.

Elbow, shoulder and spinal history require exercise substitution rather than bravado. Supported positions,

controlled tempo and range limited by symptoms are the connective-tissue strategy. Tart cherry and milk thistle in the night stage are recorded as used; their contribution cannot be isolated from sleep and load

management.

Chapter 11 - Hormonal and metabolic interpretation

An afternoon testosterone value is a trough, not a diagnosis.

The March 2026 testosterone of 7.4 nmol/L drawn at 13:28 is a non-diagnostic afternoon trough. Endocrine

Society and American Urological Association guidance requires two fasting morning draws (07:00-09:30) to

diagnose deficiency, because diurnal decline can halve afternoon values in younger men and blunts with age but does not disappear.

Metabolic interpretation in this system is conservative: body fat below 15 percent is an aspiration, not a

prerequisite for insulin sensitivity. Meal enzymes, fibre and meal sequencing are logged as tolerance tools.

Any hormonal intervention is a clinical decision outside this Encyclopedia's authority.

Analysis 11A. NMN (175 mg), boron (3 mg) and TESTOFX are recorded as used. None carries human

evidence sufficient to promise testosterone restoration. The Encyclopedia records; it does not prescribe

endocrine therapy.

Chapter 12 - The singularity philosophy

Durability outranks comparison.

The singularity in Project 100 is not a physique borrowed from a stage division. It is the point where

measurement, training, digestion, sleep and laboratory honesty operate as one system, sustained for years.

The author's own comparable measurements, function and health are the scoreboard.

"Train Like Two. Eat for Two" survives as the motto of effort. The mature reading is narrower and stronger:

train with intent, eat with record, rest without guilt, and let symptoms - not ambition - set the ceiling on any

given day. A larger stack, longer session or bigger meal does not prove better adaptation.

This philosophy chapter closes the operational book. The parts that follow - vectors, cascades, margins and laboratory playbook - are

the reference engine that keeps the philosophy honest.

Annex II - Operator checklists and red-flag checklist

Daily operator checklist

Sleep hours and awakenings recorded. Caffeine total estimated including tea.

Branch selected correctly: training grid only on training days.

R-ALA swallowed whole with water before V4 (training days).

Stool, pain, weakness and performance noted in one line each.

Optional items (Medi-C, Hey Relax) marked as taken or skipped - never assumed.

Weekly review (15 minutes)

Performance trend stable or improving? Hardware locks quiet?

One variable changed at most. Next review date set.

Red-flag checklist - for clinician discussion. Stop and seek assessment for:

Progressive hand or limb weakness, new numbness, bowel or bladder change.

Jaundice, dark urine with pale stool, fever with abdominal pain.

Recurrent watery stools with weight loss, oily stools, significant abdominal pain.

Palpitations, fainting, unusual slow pulse, muscle twitching with GI cramps (cholinergic watch).

Recurrent sweating, tremor or confusion after training (hypoglycaemia watch on R-ALA days).

Sleep disruption that does not resolve when caffeine is pulled earlier.

Bring this Encyclopedia, the current product labels and the laboratory playbook (Part VI) to the appointment. The clinician needs the

stack as recorded, not as remembered.
