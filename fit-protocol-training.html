Chapter 6 - Training mechanics and movement classes

Movement classes are defined by direction of force, joint angles and loading demands - distinct from intake vectors V1-V10.

Supported rows, presses and machine-based work dominate where hardware locks require spinal

protection. Free axial loading is progressed conservatively. Tempo, setup repeatability and pain response are recorded for every primary movement. The 2026 ACSM update supports individualized resistance

training prescription; it does not endorse any single Project 100 template for every reader.

Chapter 9 - Planning over months and years

Quarterly review beats weekly overreaction.

Quarterly body-composition review is the project cadence. The timing and scope of blood testing follow

clinical need, not the training calendar. A few comparable weigh-ins describe a week; an isolated SMM or

body-fat reading should not trigger a new diet or supplement change.

Planning horizons: weekly (performance, sleep, digestion log), quarterly (standardized BIA trend, waist,

photos under consistent light), annually (full laboratory panel timed with the clinician, medication and

supplement reconciliation). Brand changes and label versions are logged with dates, because a product

name is not a formula guarantee.

Analysis 9A. Adaptation is slow and noisy. The planning system exists to prevent the most common

error in self-directed programmes: changing three variables after one noisy measurement and learning

nothing. One change, one review window, one decision.

Chapter 10 - Connective tissue and injury considerations

Collagen, vitamin C, glycine and load progression support tolerance; no supplement proves nerve healing.

Daily collagen exposure is substantial and deliberate: 10 g marine collagen in V3, 10 g in V5 on rest days,

plus bovine collagen in legacy meal modules and glycine 3-5 g plus glutamine 5 g in V10. Vitamin C (including optional Medi-C) is the required co-factor for collagen cross-linking. The system pairs substrate

with progressive, symptom-guided loading - the stimulus that actually directs tissue adaptation.

Elbow, shoulder and spinal history require exercise substitution rather than bravado. Supported positions,

controlled tempo and range limited by symptoms are the connective-tissue strategy. Tart cherry and milk thistle in the night stage are recorded as used; their contribution cannot be isolated from sleep and load

management.
