08Field Notes · Evidence Audit

Gym Myths, Corrected

Five locker-room claims checked against the evidence and replaced with the clinically accurate read.

Myth → Corrected Read
  • "Foamy urine proves excess protein." — Foam is usually flow speed and toilet-bowl chemistry; persistent foam is worth a conversation with a clinician, not an automatic dose cut.
  • "Heavy post-meal digestion means the meal was too big." — High-fat meals slow gastric emptying by design; sluggishness alone isn't a kidney or liver signal.
  • "A clicking elbow means joint damage." — Painless clicking is usually tendon or gas-bubble movement; it only needs attention if paired with swelling, locking, or pain.
  • "A tight lower back after lifting means a disc problem." — Muscular tightness after loaded hinge work is common and self-resolving; red flags are numbness, radiating pain, or weakness, not tightness alone.
  • "High-protein diets damage healthy kidneys." — In people with normal kidney function, higher protein intake isn't established as harmful; the caution applies specifically to pre-existing kidney disease.
