Origins: The Architecture of Return

▸
Version-audit note corrected Sep 17, 2026. The origin date in every prior edition (2019) was wrong — the true starting point, confirmed directly by the source, is 2010. This chapter is rebuilt around five distinct eras rather than a single "before/after the interruption" split, and the three separate origin dates — when the work began, when it started being documented, and when the current rebuild began — are no longer conflated.

The system now called Project 100 has three points of origin, not one: the year the learning began, the year documentation began, and the year the current rebuild began. Collapsing them into a single "2019" start erased fifteen years of process. They are kept separate here, across five eras.

2010–2013 — The Beginning

The starting point was 2010, at 30 years old — not a training program, but the decision to become someone who trains. There is no documentation from this window: no logs, no photographs, no structured plan. It was pure acquisition — learning the fundamentals of lifting, nutrition, and recovery from a standing start, mostly through trial, error, and self-directed study.

2013–2016 — Progressing

2013 marks the start of documentation, not the start of the journey — the first meal logs, training records, and photographic progression archives. This era is where habits solidified: life began to structure itself around gym and work, and fitness became a stated, primary goal rather than an occasional pursuit.

2016–2018 — The Golden Era

Knowledge deepened and technique sharpened. This is the highest-output window of the first build — pushing limits and boundaries, and reaching what remains, on record, the best physical condition of the pre-interruption years. The first construction of Project 100 was built on acceleration — escalating metrics, aggressive optimization, progressive control over intake, load, and structural discipline. Skeletal muscle mass increased. Partitioning tightened. Mechanical strength advanced. It was precise, documented, and ambitious.

2018–2025 — The Dark Stage

But ambition alone does not create permanence. The interruption between 2018 and 2025 did not destroy the system; it exposed its vulnerabilities. When daily mechanical resistance was removed, structure dissolved gradually — not catastrophically, incrementally. The absence of consistent training altered endocrine rhythm. Sleep architecture shifted. Neural stimulation moved from iron to screen. Muscle protein turnover slowed. Basal metabolic rate recalibrated downward.

The Pivot occurred at 45 years of age. The data were clear: 197.1 lb total body weight, 88.2 lb skeletal muscle mass, and 22.9% body fat. These numbers did not indicate dysfunction. They indicated equilibrium — the adapted baseline of a body conditioned by reduced mechanical demand.

2025–2026 and Beyond — The Comeback

This is not a repeat of the 2016 build. The version of this system running now carries more knowledge, more precision, and more self-correction than any earlier era — including the years lost to the interruption, and mistakes made even during the productive years before it. Supplement timing, macro ceilings, and mineral synergy windows are calculated exactly, not estimated. On a 5'7" frame, the difference between 90 lb and 100 lb of skeletal muscle mass is structural, not cosmetic. An additional 10 lb of contractile tissue alters leverage, glycogen storage capacity, resting metabolic rate, and force potential. The objective became explicit: 100 lb skeletal muscle mass sustained below 15% body fat, with endocrine stability and connective durability preserved — permanence, not just peak performance.

Why the timeline matters this isn't a two-year story. Measured against most men of the same age and training profile — and against the permanent spinal, nerve, digestive, and dermal constraints documented in Ch.3 — the fifteen years before the current rebuild are load-bearing context, not backstory. This record functions as both a personal portfolio and a dated, evidence-based account of the process and the person behind it.
