Training, five lenses

Lens 1 — Physiology.

  • At 46, muscle protein synthesis responds less to the same leucine dose (anabolic resistance) while tendon collagen synthesis lags further behind; the training answer is not more load but better-distributed load: supported variations (chest-supported rows, supported presses) keep mechanical tension on muscle while removing axial spinal cost at L4-S1, so the tissue that adapts slowest sets the progression rate.

Lens 2 — Measurement.

  • Progression is judged across weeks of logged, repeatable sessions — not single heroic efforts.
  • The 10:30 band deload (100 reps) is itself a measurement: joint-friendly blood-flow volume that tells me whether the locks stayed quiet after the main session.

Lens 3 — Training.

  • Use the current six-bundle directory above.
  • Band counts vary; trainer-managed resistance, working range and progression replace the retired level schema.
  • A label sum is not measured hand force or a hardware working-load rating.

Lens 4 — Nutrition interface.

  • Training windows are fed on schedule (V2 intra-session, V4 reload with 19 g carbohydrate) so that load decisions are never confounded by under-fueling; the post-cholecystectomy pacing rule (smaller fat portions, meal enzymes) is part of the training plan, not an afterthought.

Lens 5 — Safety. Hardware locks are non-negotiable: rack pulls above patellar height, neutral grips for the left ulnar nerve, braced belt work unloading erectors 20-40%.

Pain is data about setup. Progressive weakness, new numbness, or bowel/bladder change stops the session — that rule outranks any program.

Load path and hardware safety

Load path and hardware safety

The five-link load path

  1. Structural anchor. The rig’s welded steel crossbar, or a non-marking soft-loop strap choked directly around it.
  2. Tie-down strap. VEHICLEX or THEFITGUY webbing extends the anchor point out to a forged steel D-ring terminal.
  3. Locking carabiner. A JRSGS 25 kN gate-lock clip closes the chain — gate faced away from the pull, locked shut.
  4. Band or handle. The working resistance — tubing, a grip handle, a rope attachment — clips in last, closest to the load.
  5. You. The working end of the chain — only ever as strong as the weakest link rigged in behind it.

Load path and hardware safety

Advantages and safety measures

Advantages

  • Every anchor point breaks down and reconfigures in seconds — nothing bolted or permanently mounted.
  • Soft-loop and tie-down straps do not mark or wear the structural steel the way a bare hook or band would.
  • Locking gates prevent the accidental unclipping a plain resistance-band loop is prone to mid-set.
  • Rated in kN and lb, so the hardware’s failure point sits well above anything the bands can pull.

Safety measures

  • Match hardware rating to the heaviest band in the line with real margin — never eyeball it as “strong enough.”
  • Carabiner gate faces away from the direction of pull, fully closed and locked, checked before every set.
  • Inspect webbing before use — any cut, fraying or UV-bleaching retires that strap immediately.
  • No daisy-chaining mismatched hardware — every link carries its own independent rating.
  • Load carabiners along the spine only — side- or gate-loading drops rated strength dramatically.
  • No shock-loading — bands are let out under control, never snapped or dropped into a sudden stop.

Ratings reflect the manufacturer specs printed on the hardware itself. This is a home-gym rig, not a certified climbing or industrial rigging system.
